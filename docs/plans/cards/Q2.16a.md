# Q2.16a: the renderer shows its navigation document before its scripts ran, and before the whole body

- goal: with `DWELLER_PROCESS=tab` or `site` the first paint of a navigation no longer waits for the whole load. Before, the renderer ran `engine.loadShared` to the end (document, sheets, scripts, frames, load events) before its first frame, and the browser sent the document only once it had all of it.
- source: the readiness list of Q2, "Speed with the flag", first bullet (Q2.4e and Q2.12b left).
- slices:
  1. the renderer loads in stages, as the window's own process does (`navigateEarly`, `advanceLoad`): parse, start the sheets, scripts and images at once, first frame when the sheets are in, then the scripts in tasks of at most 50 ms with input taken between them, then the frames and the load events. Done, 2026-11-16.
  2. the browser sends a navigation's body as it arrives. Done, 2026-11-16.
  3. the renderer shows what has arrived of the body before its end. Done, 2026-11-16.
- design, slice 1:
  - engine: `loadSharedParsed` is `loadShared` without `runDocument` (the page has `scripts_pending`, as `loadParsed`'s); both use `fetchShared`. The steps are the window's own (`startEarly`, `loadsIn`, `stepPageScripts`, `scriptsRan`).
  - renderer: `TabRenderer.staged` (`StagedLoad`: the sheet and script loads, `on`, `painted`, `stepping`). `load` fetches and parses (`fetchDocument`), adopts the page and calls `stageLoad`; `advanceStaged` (each loop turn, from `answerAll` and `runTimers`) is the window's `advanceLoad`: nothing before the sheets are in, then the first frame, then `stepStaged` (50 ms of scripts a turn), and at the end `sayLoaded` (the `loaded` message, as before at the end of the load). `paintWaits`: no frame before the sheets are in; while the scripts run, a frame only for an input that waits for its ack. `runTimers` runs no timer while the scripts run (as the window). `reportPage` reports nothing of the page (title, history, script navigations) until `loaded`, so the browser's checks of a loading tab see what they saw before. `finishStaged` runs the rest of a staged load at once before an agent command and before the next `navigate`. A renderer without buffers (a hosted headless Space) loads with `loadShared`, as before.
  - the scroll: the first frame is at the entry's scroll; at the end the page keeps the scroll the person gave it while it loaded, unless the entry restores one.
  - no new IPC message. `loaded` still means "committed and loaded".
- Chromium read: `third_party/blink/renderer/core/loader/document_loader.cc` (`CommitNavigation`, then `HandleData` feeds the parser while the loader runs), `third_party/blink/renderer/core/html/parser/html_document_parser.cc` (the parser yields between scripts and chunks, `PumpTokenizerIfPossible`; a parser-blocking script holds the parser, not the frame). Dweller takes: the document is shown once its sheets are in, and its scripts are tasks of the renderer's loop.

- design, slices 2 and 3:
  - net: `navigationHead(alloc, id)` gives the response head (status, type, headers, final URL; empty body) of a running navigation once the first body bytes of a 2xx hop without a Basic challenge are in, and stores that hop's cookies and HSTS then (`storeHead`, `Job.stored`, so `finishRequest` does not store them twice); `navigationBody(id)` is the body so far (libcurl decodes it). A redirect, a 401 and an error status are never given early.
  - browser: `answerHeld` calls `streamNavigation` for a held navigation that still runs: the first time (`startStreamed`) it makes the checks `sendNavigation` makes at the end, on the head (`streamsHere`: `framingRefused`; the site of the tab's lock, taking the lock when there is none; `coopSwapsTab`). A response that needs a process swap or is refused is not streamed (`HeldFetch.whole`) and goes the old way, whole. Else `sendHead` (the head half of `sendFetched`: committed URL, top, COOP, `fetch_head`) and `sendBody` of the new bytes each round (`HeldFetch.sent`); at the end `endStreamed` sends the rest and `fetch_end`. A transfer that fails after its head was sent ends with `fetch_end`: the renderer shows the part it has, as Chromium does for a cut body. No new IPC message: `fetch_head`, `fetch_body`, `fetch_end` in the same order, earlier.
  - renderer: while `fetchDocument` waits (`awaitFetch` of the navigation, `Preview.on`, `TabRenderer.outer` = its id), `previewArrived` runs each turn: 100 ms after the head, if the body has not ended and is `text/html`, `buildPreview` parses the bytes up to the last `>` into a page that never runs scripts (`engine.previewPage`: `pageOf` with `scripts_pending`, not attached to the browsing context) and starts its sheets, scripts and images (`startEarly`: the loads the real page asks for next are then in the browser's cache); once its sheets are in, `showPreview` paints it with `paintFrame` at scroll 0 in place of the old page. A later preview is built when more bytes came, no sooner than 200 ms or four times the last one's cost. The wait reads the socket with `previewWait`'s timeout; `scanReplies` leaves the navigation's own replies for the outer wait when a nested synchronous fetch scans (layout can fetch), and takes `frame_ack` during the wait (`previewAck`) so a second preview has a buffer. Every other message stays in the inbox until the document is in, as before. At the end the preview is dropped (`Preview.end`) and the real page loads in stages.
  - found by the merge with Q2.14d (`settled`): a staged load starts the page's images in the background (`preloadImages`), and the renderer never laid out when they arrived, so the page never went idle. `imagesIn` (from `paintFrame`) marks a frame due when a decode landed or an image arrived (at most each 100 ms), and `timerWait` polls at 8 ms while images load or decode, as the window does.

- 2026-11-19, `window_renderer_loads_parts` failed once in an integration pass (wave branch at 492db3e0 with lane perf-style's Q4.6d merged; that change has no renderer code and is not the cause). Cause: the test's page, not the product. The first part's background image was `/held-now?p`, which releases the held rest; the preview asks for it, and the real page asks for it again, and that second request waits at the server (nothing is held any more) for its 30 s limit. The window's state query (`--text`, lane proc-tests' `sayState`) waits for running loads up to 5 s, so the run took 9 s and its text could come back empty: 20 of 20 runs by hand on the wave tip showed the 9 s and no text, loaded or not; the suite's run passed or failed by which side gave up first. Fix: the server's `/parts/NAME?KEY` now sends its rest once `/parts-go?KEY` was asked for, and `/parts-go` answers at once every time; the page's image is `/parts-go?p`. The test's expectations are unchanged. After: 20 of 20 runs in 0.26 s at most on a quiet machine, 20 of 20 in 0.51 s at most beside 12 busy loops on 4 cores.

## Status: done, 2026-11-16

Left:
- `loaded` is sent at the end of the load, so the address bar's committed URL, the title and the history entry change then, not at the first frame; Chromium commits first (`DidCommitNavigation`) and reports the load's end apart. A preview is shown while the browser still counts the tab as loading the old entry.
- the old page does not run while the navigation document is fetched, and input sent meanwhile waits in the inbox for the new page (as before, and as in the window's own process); Stop and a new URL typed during the fetch still wait for it.
- a preview is a second parse of the prefix, not an incremental parse: each preview parses from the start, and the real page parses the whole document again. Its sheets reach the real page through the browser's cache (one more IPC round trip each), not from the preview.
- a document is not streamed when its response needs a process swap (a cross-site navigation of a locked tab, COOP), is a frame's that could be refused, or is not 2xx; those wait for the whole body as before. The first navigation of a new tab and same-site navigations stream.
- no flow control: the browser's out buffer takes the body as fast as the network gives it (at most the 32 MB document limit, as before).
- a preview cut inside an unclosed `<style>` or `<script>` shows nothing after it until the next preview.
