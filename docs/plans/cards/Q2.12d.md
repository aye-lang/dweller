# Q2.12d: same-site popups in their opener's renderer

- goal: with `DWELLER_SPACES=renderer`, a popup that keeps its opener and is same-site with it is created in its opener's renderer process, as Chromium does, so `window.opener`, `window.name` and synchronous scripting between the two work as in process; the golden agent session `popup` then passes byte-equal (`opener set name w1`) and `window_spaces_in_renderer` runs all 9 sessions. The same code serves popups of the person's tabs under `DWELLER_PROCESS=tab`.
- source: Q2.12's Left, third item; the flip's readiness list ("`opener`, named targets and popup openers across renderers"); `docs/process-model.md` §3 reuse rule 1 ("the same BCG and the same site: its one process (required)").
- why it is three slices: a renderer serves exactly one tab today (one connection, one `TabRenderer` on the stack of `serveOn`, the engine's hooks bound to it), and the browser's `TabProcess` is one connection that owns its pid (`endTabProcess` waits for that pid to exit; a crash is seen per `TabProcess`). Both sides must learn "one process, several tabs" before a popup can be placed in its opener's process.
- slices:
  1. The renderer serves several tabs (done, below). IPC 74 `attach_tab{context}` B→R on the opener's connection carries one socket fd (SCM_RIGHTS, as `resize` carries its buffers); the renderer starts a second `TabRenderer` on that socket that shows popup context `context` (the one its page opened, with its opener and name). One loop polls every tab's connection; the engine's hooks act for the tab being served. Nothing sends 74 yet, so no behaviour changes. Verify: check, unit tests (74 round-trips, goes only to the renderer), taste, layers; `window_renderer`, `window_spaces agent_sessions window_renderer_lifecycle window_login_in_renderer`: 0 failed.
  2. The browser places same-site popups that keep an opener in the opener's process. `Context` says whether the new context keeps its opener (Chromium's `opener_suppressed`); `openedSlot` (hosted Spaces) and the person's tab-mode popups call `attachTabProcess(opener_tp, slot, context)` when the opener is kept and `href`'s site is the opener's committed site in the same partition: a socketpair, 74 with one end, a `TabProcess` whose `host` is the opener's (pid, site lock, partition shared; `endTabProcess` of an attached connection sends `shutdown` and closes only its fd; the pid is waited for when its last connection ends; `pollTabSlot` reaps a shared pid once and marks every tab on it gone). Otherwise a renderer of its own as today. Verify: `window_spaces_in_renderer` 9 of 9 (the `popup` filter removed); `window_spaces agent_sessions window_renderer_lifecycle window_login_in_renderer` and `window_renderer`: 0 failed; a new `window_renderer_popup_opener` (`DWELLER_PROCESS=tab`): a same-site `window.open` popup sees its opener, sets `opener.document.title`, and runs in the opener's pid; a `noopener` and a cross-site popup each get a pid of their own with `opener` null.
  3. Edges of a shared process: an attached tab that navigates cross-site leaves the process (the speculative swap must not end the shared pid; its `opener` becomes null), the opener's tab closes while its popup lives (the process stays), a crash of the shared process fails the requests of every hosted tab on it with "renderer crashed", and `takeContext`'s gesture check for a context request of the attached tab. Verify: one window test per edge, plus the slice 2 tests.
- design as built (slice 1):
  - renderer: `serveOn` keeps a list of `TabRenderer`s on the heap (`newTabRenderer`) and hands it to `serveTabs`: a tab with a whole message waiting is served first (`waitingTab`), else `pollTabs` waits on every tab's connection for at most the nearest timer of any tab (`timerWait`), and on a timeout runs every tab's timers. Before a tab's messages or timers run, `serveAs` points the engine's hooks (fetch, cookies and storage, remote windows, the hang hook) at it. `shutdown`, a bad message or a closed connection ends that tab only (`endTab`, which now closes its connection); the process exits with its last tab, as before for one.
  - `attach_tab{context}` queues the next received fd with the context (`TabRenderer.attaching`); after the batch, `adoptAttached` starts a `TabRenderer` on it whose `context` is set, so its first `navigate` loads into that context (`load` → `setLoadingContext`) and the page has the opener and name its opener gave it. It sends `hello` on the new connection, as a fresh renderer does. The fd is closed instead when the context is unknown, has no opener, already shows a page, is loose, or is another tab's (`attachable`).
  - net's remote loads are keyed process-wide now (`PendingFetch.load`, from `next_load`), since two tabs' wire ids both start at 1; `cancelRemote` and `landFetched` translate.
- IPC: 74 `attach_tab{context}` B→R, with one fd. 75–77 unused.
- references: `content/browser/renderer_host/render_frame_host_impl.cc` `CreateNewWindow` (read in the local checkout: when the opener is suppressed or script access is not allowed "we should open the window in a new BrowsingInstance, and thus a new process"; otherwise the new window's main frame is created for the opener's renderer, which made it), `docs/process_model_and_site_isolation.md` ("Principal Instance": "Any two documents with the same principal in the same browsing context group ... must live in the same process"; "Browsing Context Group": popups with `window.opener` references belong to it).
- files (slice 1): new `browser/ipc/attach-tab_test.aye`, `browser/renderer/{attach,new-tab-renderer,serve-tabs,poll-tabs,serve-as,end-tab,adopt-attached,attachable}.aye`; changed `browser/ipc/{message,decode,put-payload,from-renderer}.aye`, `browser/renderer/{serve-on,tab-renderer,answer-all,pending-fetch,start-remote,cancel-remote,land-fetched}.aye`.

- design as built (slice 2):
  - `Context` (64) carries `keeps` (0 or 1): the renderer says whether the new context has an opener (`send-contexts.aye`); the browser refuses any other value as `bad context`. A renderer that lies only moves its own same-site popup.
  - `attachSource` (ui) decides: the context keeps its opener, the opener's process lives and is a tab's (not a child frame's), and `href`'s site (`writeSite`) is the site of the opener's committed URL. Then `openedSlot` gives the new slot `attachTabProcess(from, slot.id)` instead of a renderer of its own (the person's tabs: `slot.process` is set before `pollTabSlot` would start one; hosted Spaces: `hostTab(ui, sp, from)`). Anything else (noopener, cross-site, a failed socketpair) gets a renderer of its own as before.
  - `attachTabProcess`: a socketpair (both ends close-on-exec), a `TabProcess` from `newTabProcess` (split out of `startTabProcess`) with the opener's pid, partition and site, and a `PendingAttach { context, fd }` on the opener. `sendAttaches` sends 74 with the fd as soon as the opener's outbox is empty (at once, else after the next flush in `serviceTabProcess`), so the browser never waits and 74 stays in order with the opener's other messages.
  - No `host` pointer: which records share a process is read from the pid (`sharesProcess` walks `tab_processes`), so no record can dangle when the other ends first. `endTabProcess` sends `shutdown`, and waits for the pid (`awaitExit`) only when no other live record has it; `dropTabProcess` kills and reaps once and marks every record of the pid gone with the same reason (a crash, a bad message or Stop of a shared process ends each tab on it, as in Chromium); the speculative swap no longer kills a shared pid (`serviceSpeculative`).
  - renderer: after a tab's batch, `reportOthers` paints and reports the other tabs of the process, since the script that just ran may have changed their pages.
  - `--state` gets one line, `renderer-tabs PID PID ...` (each of the person's tabs' renderer pid, 0 for none), for the test.

- design as built (slice 3):
  - Found by the first edge test: closing the opener's tab killed the shared renderer with SIGPIPE (a write to the closed connection), and any failed write would have ended the process (`serveTabs` returned 1). The renderer now ignores SIGPIPE (`ignoreSigpipe`, before the sandbox) and a tab whose serving fails (a closed connection, a bad message, an error in its timers or its report) is marked `broken` and ended alone (`endBroken`); the process exits with its last tab.
  - The other edges needed no new code beyond slice 2's pid-based sharing: a shared pid is not killed by the speculative swap, `endTabProcess` of one tab leaves the process to the other, `dropTabProcess` marks every tab of the pid gone, and each tab's `TabProcess` has its own input sequence, so `takeContext`'s gesture check works per tab.

## Status: done, 2026-11-16

Left:
- A cross-site popup that keeps its opener needs a remote `opener` proxy in the popup's renderer and the popup as a remote window in the opener's (Q2.11's proxies are for frames of one tab; `postTarget` finds only frames of the sender's tab); its `window.name` does not reach its renderer either (`window_renderer_popup_opener`: `name` is empty for `w2`). The readiness list's "`opener` and named targets across renderers".
- A popup opened with an empty URL or `about:blank` has no site, so it gets a renderer of its own (Chromium keeps it in the opener's process).
- In a shared renderer, the engine's hooks act for the tab being served: a load a script of one tab starts in the other tab's document goes over the first tab's connection, and a cancel of it from the other tab is not found (the reply lands and is dropped).
- A write into another page's document (`opener.document.title = ...`) does not raise that page's script version, so its tab's title (and the browser's `title`) follows only at that page's next own DOM change; the same in one process (`engine/script.aye` `afterTask`).
- `window_renderer_popup_opener` runs with `DWELLER_PROCESS=tab` only; `site` is the proc-tests lane's sweep.
- A popup that leaves its opener's process loses `opener` and its `window.name` (the new renderer starts a fresh context), and its opener sees it as closed; Chromium keeps both through proxies. With the readiness list's "popups kept across a renderer swap" [Q2.6b].
- A hang or a bad message of one tab ends every tab of a shared process (Chromium does the same); the "not responding" bar shows on the tab that is shown.
