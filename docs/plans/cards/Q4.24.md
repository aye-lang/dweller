# Q4.24: where safe mode's cost in the JS VM goes, and what it would take to win it back

- goal: say, per source line and per kind of check, where the shipped `--mode safe` build spends its extra instructions in the bytecode VM (`vmLoop`), and measure two ways to win them back: A, check each function's bytecode once and read its operands unchecked in the dispatch loop; B, rewrite the reads so the compiler needs fewer checks and nothing is trusted. This is a measurement card: no code of the prototypes is merged.
- cause: Q0.2/Q1.1 shipped safe at +17.6% on jsbench (`browser/engine/PERF.md`, Q0.2). Aye O7.7 (aye-lang/aye#31) leaves out the checks that loops prove, but 77% of navier-stokes's instructions are in `vmLoop`, whose indexes come from the bytecode, so jsbench did not move: with every bounds check off safe still ran 1.26 times fast's instructions.
- method: `valgrind --tool=callgrind` (3.22), instruction counts of `dweller js` on `navier-stokes.js` and `richards.js` at `SMOKE=10` (the scripts are made by `jsbench.prepare`, as `jsbench.py --smoke` does), `browser/build.sh --mode safe|fast` of 7d53dba with aye c8f7909. Line numbers need line info: the fast build for the line diff is `aye build browser --mode fast --cflags -g` (same instruction counts as the shipped fast build to 5 digits: 1,610,695,606 against 1,610,695,641). `browser/tools/callgrind_lines.py SAFE.out FAST.out` prints the per-function and per-line diff; `browser/tools/jsbench_modes.py NAME=BIN... [-- WORKLOAD...]` runs several builds interleaved (wall and CPU time, median and minimum).
- design: nothing merged. The prototypes are local branches: `q4-24-proto` (A, 714fd2c and fe5ff6d on 7d53dba) and `q4-24-proto-b` (B, 181de60 on 7d53dba).

Classification of the 702 M navier-stokes gap, from the above:

- Bounds checks are most of it. The operand and opcode reads (`bytes[pc]`, `bytes[at + k]`, `regs[base + r]`, `consts[k]`, `feedback[i]`) and the pc arithmetic are what A removes: 479 M, 68% of the gap (richards: 113 M of 175 M, 64%). On their own the cast and overflow checks of the same reads are worth nothing: B removes them and the run gets 2.6% slower (O7.7: cast checks off -62 M, overflow checks off +51 M, both inside layout noise).
- The decoders matter as code layout. `vmImm`, `vmIdx` and especially `vmWide` (4099 bytes of C in safe, 3177 in fast) are too big for `SMALL_FN` (1024 bytes: `compile` in aye's `driver.rs` makes a function `static inline` in the shared header only below that, because without LTO clang inlines only within a unit), so each read of a wide operand was a call: `vmWide` was 53.5 M of richards's gap. Splitting `vmWide` into `vmWide2` and `vmWide4` and checking nothing in `vmIdx` took A from 1,934.7 M to 1,834.0 M (navier-stokes) and 562.4 M to 479.6 M (richards). Thin LTO on the safe build does not do this (navier-stokes +1%, richards -8%): the cost is in the checked bodies, not in the missing inlining across units.
- The rest is in the callees, not in the bytecode. In safe `vmKeyedDenseElem` (7410 bytes of C) is called, 326 M; fast inlines it (149 M in its lines). With E2 (its checks, `Heap.obj`'s, `vmContextCell`'s and `vmEnvAt`'s gone) navier-stokes is at fast. Richards keeps +10.7% in E2: call and return (`vmPushCall`, `List(VmSaved)`, `List(Frame)`, `vmIcHitProp`), whose indexes come from interpreter state.
- Per bytecode instruction: safe 65.0 machine instructions, fast 45.3, A 51.6.

## 2. Prototype A (`q4-24-proto`)

- `browser/js/bc-validate.aye`, `bc-insn-end.aye`, `bc-insn-ok.aye` (131 lines): `bcValidate(code)` runs at the end of `compileFunction`, once per function, with the decoder `bcDecode` and the operand kinds of `BC_INFO` (BYTECODE.md section 2). It checks that
  - every instruction, with its `Wide`/`ExtraWide` prefix and operands, lies inside the code and is not a bare prefix or an unknown opcode;
  - every `Reg`/`RegOut` operand is in `-(nparams + 1) .. nregs` (the receiver, the parameters and the frame's registers); the first register of the ranges of `CallProperty`, `CallUndefinedReceiver`, `Construct`, `CallWithSpread`, `TemplateString`, `CreateClass`, `SuperCall`, `SuspendGenerator` and `ResumeGenerator` is not checked (the range is empty for a call without arguments, and the first register can be `nregs`), and those handlers keep their checked `regs[..]`;
  - every `Const` operand is below `consts.len`, every `Slot` below `feedback.len`;
  - every jump (immediate, through the constant pool, `JumpLoop`) and every `SwitchOnSmi` table entry lands on the start of an instruction (prefix included), and every handler's target does;
  - the last instruction is `Return`, `Throw`, `Jump`, `JumpConstant` or `JumpLoop`, so execution cannot run off the end.
  A function that fails is a compiler bug and `unreachable`s at compile time, naming the line, before it runs.
- `browser/js/vm-unchecked.aye`: `vmReg`, `vmSetReg`, `vmRegIndex`, `vmRegGet`, `vmRegPut`, `vmOp`, `vmConstAt`, `vmFeedback`, `vmJump`, `vmJumpBack`: each one `@setSafety(false)` and one expression. `vmImm`, `vmIdx` and `vmWide` (split in two) also skip their checks. In `vmLoop` the register reads and writes of every instruction but `SuspendGenerator` and `ResumeGenerator`, the opcode fetch, the constant and feedback reads and the jumps use them; `pc += n` became `pc = pc +% n` (wrapping cannot trap, the validated pc cannot wrap). Nothing else in `vmLoop` lost a check: the `regs[fp]` spills, `regs[r]` of the calls and everything behind a pointer keep theirs. In C, the checks in `vmLoop` fall from 344/158/124/96 (overflow/index/cast/slice) to 69/97/51/96.
- `browser/js/unit_tests.aye`: three tests (`bytecode: bcValidate ...`): a register of the frame is accepted and one beyond `nregs` is not; a constant outside the pool and a function that ends without `Return` are not; a jump into the middle of an instruction (a patched operand) is not.
- Cost of validating: 6.9 M instructions of the 4.27 G that `three.js` runs at SMOKE=10 (0.16%: `bcValidate`, `bcInsnOk`, `bcOperandOk`, `bcInsnEnd` and `bcDecode`); 0.4 M of navier-stokes's 1.8 G.
- Correctness (A, `fe5ff6d`): `aye check browser` clean; `aye test browser` 287 passed (the 3 new); `cargo test --release --test browser -- aye_unit_tests js_conformance jsdom_fixtures` 4 passed; `test262.py run test/built-ins/Array test/language/statements -j 2` (test262 d53ebc8b19) 9366/12413 on A and on the safe build, the two result files identical (Array 2492/3067, 16 skipped; statements 6874/9346, 1 skipped); `jsbench.py --smoke` on A: every checksum ok; every workload of the suite also compared its output with the golden in all the timing runs below. All 293 scripts of `browser/tests/js` pass the validator (the first run rejected `SuperCall` and `TemplateString` with an empty range, which is why the range operands are exempt).

## 3. Prototype B (`q4-24-proto-b`, 181de60)

Taking the reads off the checks the compiler cannot remove, with no trust added: unsigned operands as `usize` (`vmUIdx`, no `i64` to `usize` cast), a register operand as `fp -% 128 +% ((b ^ 128) as usize)` for a one-byte operand (the sign flip of the byte is the index, so no cast, no add overflow), `pc` by wrapping arithmetic. The bounds checks stay. Result: no gain, slightly worse (table above: 2,374.1 M and 603.2 M against 2,313.1 M and 592.6 M, instructions; wall 1.06 and 1.00 of safe by minimum). Two things it showed: a helper whose C is over 1024 bytes is never inlined (`vmRegAt` at 1031 bytes was 605 M of calls on its own; split in two it is 765 bytes and inlines), and a cast or an add overflow check costs about one instruction against the bounds check beside it, which is what the loop pays per operand either way.

What B cannot do: prove `regs[fp + r]` in range. The one provable form would be a fixed-size window: `win: *[256]Value` over `stack[fp - 128 ..]` indexed by `(byte ^ 128) as usize`, which clang knows is below 256, with 128 padding slots below the first frame and above the top of the stack so the window is always inside the allocation. Then an operand outside the frame reads or writes a neighbouring frame's slot (a wrong JS value, never memory outside the stack), with no validator. It is not built: it touches every `truncate`, `vmPushFrame`, `vmEnterHandler` and the stack's growth, and it covers only the 1-byte operands; the `bytes[pc]` checks (two per instruction) stay.

## 5. Security argument for A

What A trusts: that the bytecode `bcValidate` accepted is the bytecode the loop runs, that `fp - nparams - 1 .. fp + nregs` stays inside `Interp.stack` while the frame runs (`vmPushFrame` pushes `nregs` slots above the arguments; every `truncate` in the VM keeps them: to `sv.top` of a caller, to `fp + nregs` in `vmEnterHandler`), and that no code writes into `BytecodeArray.code` after `compileFunction` (nothing does: the builder owns it before `finish`).

What a page controls: JavaScript, not bytecode. The only way to an invalid operand is a bug in `compiler.aye` that emits one for some construct, and the safe build turns that into a panic at the first use of the register. With A it turns into a panic at compile time (validator) or, if the validator has a hole too, into an out-of-bounds read or write of `Interp.stack`'s buffer, a jump to a wrong offset of the code buffer, or a read of `consts` or `feedback` past their end, in the renderer process. A hole needs both bugs: the compiler emitting a bad operand and the validator accepting it. The validator is 131 lines of structure checks over a table (`BC_INFO`), is exercised by every function of every test (293 scripts, the jsdom fixtures, 12.4 k test262 files, jsbench), and can be fuzzed against the safe VM as an oracle: any random byte string it accepts must run without a panic in safe mode (not done here).

The blast radius of a hole is the renderer, which Q2.8 sandboxes (a compromised renderer has no file, network or process authority of its own). That is the position of V8: Ignition's handlers (`src/interpreter/interpreter-generator.cc`, not re-read for this card) address a register operand from the frame pointer with no bounds check in a release build; `BytecodeArray::BytecodeArrayVerify` (`src/diagnostics/objects-debug.cc`) still has "TODO: walk bytecodes and immediate values to validate sanity"; since 2025 `src/sandbox/bytecode-verifier.{h,cc}` has `VerifyLight` (jump and switch targets, handler ranges: flag `verify_bytecode_light`, default false) and `VerifyFull` (also registers, parameters, constant-pool indexes and ids: `verify_bytecode_full`, default `DEBUG_BOOL`, and used in fuzzing), a failure is `FATAL`, and with the V8 sandbox the `BytecodeArray` lives in trusted space outside the sandbox so a corrupted heap cannot rewrite it. V8 relies on the generator being right and, if it is not, on the sandbox; it does not run the full verifier in release builds. A would verify fully in release, which is stricter than V8's default, and keeps a smaller, checked part of the loop trusting the result.

What A does not cover: ids that come from data, not from bytecode. `Heap.obj(o)` indexes the object table with an id from a `Value`; `vmKeyedDenseElem` indexes `ob.elems` with a JS number. Those are the remaining ~250 M of navier-stokes (E2). They are protected by GC and heap invariants, not by a load-time check, so unchecking them needs a different argument (and the GC-stress runs of BYTECODE.md section 1) and is not proposed here.

## 6. Recommendation

1. Do A, scoped as prototyped, as its own step (Q4.24b): the validator, `bcValidate` unit tests with a hand-made bad function per rule, the helpers in `vm-unchecked.aye`, and the `vmLoop` edit; keep the safe mode's checks everywhere else. It recovers about two thirds of the instruction gap on both benchmarks and of the suite's time, at 0.16% instructions for validating and a trust cost that is one 131-line file plus the invariant of 5. Do the fuzz oracle of 5 with it (random byte strings and mutated real functions must either be rejected or run to completion in safe mode without a panic), and make a failing validation a compile-time `unreachable` as in the prototype.
2. Do not take B: it only moves instructions around. Keep its two lessons: Aye's cross-unit inlining stops at 1024 bytes of generated C (`SMALL_FN`), and a hot helper in `vmLoop` should be checked for that (`emit-c | wc`); and `@setSafety(false)` applies to the function it is in, not to what it calls.
3. Ask Aye for a cheaper check, not for more trust: the remaining 14% is bounds checks in `vmKeyedDenseElem` and `Heap.obj` and the call/return path. A hint such as `@assumeLen` or a `[]T` field that records `len <= cap` would let `ob.elems[i]` after `i < ob.nelems` be proved; that is an aye-lang card, and until it exists the choice is E2 (unchecked, with the heap-invariant argument above) or leaving them.
4. The plan's 5% target for jsbench safe/fast cannot be met by 1 alone (A: 1.14 and 1.15 of fast in instructions; the suite's wall about 1.05 of fast); with 3 it is within reach, and richards would still be 10% over because of call and return.

- references: V8 `src/sandbox/bytecode-verifier.{h,cc}` (VerifyLight, VerifyFull, what they check; Dweller takes the list of rules, not the code), `src/flags/flag-definitions.h` (`verify_bytecode_light`, `verify_bytecode_full` defaults), `src/diagnostics/objects-debug.cc` (`BytecodeArrayVerify` and its TODO), `src/objects/bytecode-array.h` (trusted space, `MarkVerified`). - files: new `docs/plans/cards/Q4.24.md`, `browser/tools/callgrind_lines.py`, `browser/tools/jsbench_modes.py`; `browser/engine/PERF.md` (section Q4.24).
